## 2. Recommended negotiation architecture

**Lead with a paid farm operations and commercial-development management mandate, not a speculative lease.**

Target commercial structure:
- Fixed monthly management retainer: **BWP 10,000 minimum**, paid monthly in arrears or preferably in advance, with scope, working days, travel, authority and reporting defined in writing.
- Initial term: 90-day paid pilot, renewable by agreement.
- Separate, pre-approved operating budget funded by the owners; Farouk should not finance inputs, wages, transport, equipment or farm running costs personally.

If the family cannot commit to BWP 10,000 fixed immediately, negotiate a shorter paid diagnostic / feasibility assignment first, with a written decision date and a defined path to the monthly management contract. Do not represent the five-day BWP 10,000 feasibility offer as a monthly income arrangement; it is a separate one-off assignment.

## 3. Commercial models to evaluate

### A. Farm operations manager / commercial farm-development manager — preferred first route
Farouk manages an agreed portfolio of farm operations and development work: operational planning, staff/task coordination, production calendars, input and inventory controls, water/irrigation records, cost tracking, buyer engagement, sales coordination, supplier comparisons and monthly performance reporting.

- Income security: High relative to other models if a fixed fee is contractually agreed and paid.
- Capital requirement for Farouk: Low, provided owners fund operations and travel.
- Main risks: Scope creep, unclear authority, unpaid expenses, unclear baseline, and being held accountable for decisions outside his control.
- Safeguards: Written job/management description, monthly retainer, owner-funded budget, expense policy, delegated authority matrix, reporting cadence and termination notice.

### B. Paid turnaround / farm-performance improvement assignment
A defined 30–60 day assignment to establish the farm's baseline: asset register, enterprise economics, labour and input controls, water-use review, crop/livestock plans, buyer map and a 12-month operating plan.

- Fee must be agreed before work begins; the prior BWP 10,000 / five-day feasibility proposal is a possible anchor only if scope is comparable.
- Do not perform extensive unpaid feasibility work.
- Deliver a concise baseline report and investment / operating decision plan.

### C. Lease or concession of a defined farm unit
Farouk leases or operates a specified plot, greenhouse, livestock unit, pack area or other asset.

- Attractive only if land access, water, infrastructure, permitted use, market and operating costs are verified.
- Prefer a rent-free mobilisation period or rent linked to actual production / sales during establishment, if the owner agrees.
- No fixed lease payment before a viable production and buyer plan is demonstrated.
- Separate the lease from the management retainer if Farouk is also managing the wider farm.

### D. Joint venture / special-purpose enterprise
The Setlhare family contributes land, water, infrastructure and/or capital; Farouk contributes management, technical coordination, market development and commercial systems.

- Establish each party's contribution, ownership, decision rights, capital calls, profit waterfall, asset ownership, exit rights and dispute process in a written agreement.
- Pay a management fee for actual work; equity or profit share is additional upside, not a substitute for the minimum monthly income.
- Avoid informal partnerships and personal guarantees.

### E. Farm commercialisation and market-access service
Farouk develops buyers, pre-orders, supply schedules, aggregation, grading/packing coordination, delivery planning and traceability. Income can be a fixed service fee plus a disclosed commission on collected sales.

- The strongest approach is buyer-led production: obtain buyer specifications, indicative volumes, quality standards, delivery windows and price terms before planting.
- Avoid growing generic vegetables on the assumption that all produce will sell. Botswana's national horticulture figures show strong growth and some oversupply in vegetables, while seasonal gaps, quality, affordability, water and market access remain constraints.
- Build a small pilot around verified buyer demand and actual water/soil capacity.

### F. Farm leisure / education / events development
Continue the Mmamashia farm-and-leisure concept as a separate, phased business-development stream, not the immediate income anchor. Start with low-capital, bookable experiences only after site suitability, permissions, sanitation, safety, insurance, water and demand are verified. Larger water-play or amusement infrastructure requires capital and should not be proposed as a near-term personal-income solution.

## 4. Enterprise screening — do not select until evidence is collected

Potential units to assess:
1. Existing farm operations improvement (first priority if an operating farm exists).
2. Buyer-backed horticulture / high-value crops using drip irrigation and a verified crop calendar.
3. Seedlings, nursery or protected-cropping services where buyer demand and water economics justify it.
4. Aggregation / packhouse / market-access services using existing growers and confirmed buyers.
5. Livestock or poultry only if existing infrastructure, reliable water, feed economics, animal-health controls and buyer channels support the case.
6. Farm leisure, educational visits and events as a later diversification stream.

Do not assume poultry is an automatic opportunity: Botswana reported poultry self-sufficiency in chicken meat and table eggs in March 2026, and feed-cost exposure remains material. Do not assume horticulture is automatically profitable: CEDA's published guidance calls for reliable water, soil/water tests and crop planning; it specifies 5–6 m3/hour per hectare and at least 0.5 ha net shade for horticulture financing cases. These are programme criteria, not a guarantee of commercial viability.

## 8. Botswana evidence and implications

- CEDA's agribusiness guidance identifies horticulture, rain-fed agriculture, cattle, small stock, poultry, piggery and related sectors, but stresses viability and subsector-specific infrastructure. Its horticulture criteria include reliable water, 5–6 m3/hour per hectare, at least 0.5 hectares net shade (subject to crop plan), high-value crops and soil/water tests.
- The Ministry's farm business-plan appraisal service requires a reliable water source, land rights, a developed business plan and a land assessment report; it indicates an approximately two-week appraisal period once documents are submitted.
- Daily News reported that Botswana's horticulture output reached approximately 140,881 tonnes from April 2025 to January 2026, with vegetables at 114,080 tonnes, slightly above estimated national demand by 1.8%; fruit met about 67% of demand. It also highlighted seasonal gaps, climate variability, water scarcity, input costs, storage and market access. The implication is to pursue quality, timing, buyer commitments and post-harvest capability rather than indiscriminate volume expansion.
- Lion Park Resort's published ticket/package pages show an established leisure competitor with full-access tickets around P90–P95 and group packages including school trips, birthdays, conferences, camping and team building. A Mmamashia concept should differentiate through farm-based experiences, educational programming, food, events and carefully tested group packages rather than copy its capital-intensive rides and attractions.
