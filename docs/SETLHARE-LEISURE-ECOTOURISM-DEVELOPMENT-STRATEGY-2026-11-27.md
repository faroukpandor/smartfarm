## 1. Executive recommendation

The meeting with Bernard Setlhare reopens an earlier idea: developing Setlhare Farms into a distinctive destination for events and ecotourism. The opportunity has now broadened. Bernard described a property arrangement said to include areas divided into approximately **60 m × 60 m plots (3,600 m² each)**, with roads between them, as well as existing conferencing, swimming-pool and garden areas. He said he would send the map and division plan. Until that material is received, these dimensions and the nature/legal status of the divisions must be treated as verbal information, not as surveyed or approved parcels.

The recommended concept is an **integrated, phased leisure, nature, recreation and events destination**, not simply a water park and not a collection of unrelated attractions. Its value should come from making the existing facilities and future activity areas work together: day visits, family recreation, school and educational outings, birthdays, corporate team-building, conferences, garden events, nature experiences and selected adventure or aquatic attractions.

Farouk should position himself as **Setlhare Farms' independent owner-side development and procurement orchestrator**. He should represent the owner's interests, translate the owner's goals into a clear project brief, coordinate independent specialists, compare supplier proposals, track cost and risk, and help sequence the development. He should not act as an undisclosed manufacturer's agent, accept hidden commissions, guarantee equipment performance, provide regulated professional design services outside his competence, or operate/market a tourism enterprise before the necessary licensing position is confirmed.

The correct sequence is:

1. Obtain the property map, survey/land documents and the owner's objectives.
2. Establish land-use, water, utilities, access, environmental, safety and licensing constraints.
3. Conduct an owner-led discovery and market-feasibility exercise.
4. Create an independent concept masterplan and a phased business case.
5. Activate existing conference, pool and garden assets with bookable products where legally and operationally possible.
6. Pilot only a small number of low-capital activities and expand from observed demand.
7. Procure major equipment only after technical design, lifecycle costs, approvals, safety responsibilities and funding are confirmed.

**Do not start by sending manufacturers a general request to show everything they sell.** That risks letting product catalogues dictate the destination, driving unnecessary capital expenditure and fragmenting the site. Setlhare Farms should first define the intended visitor, experience, capacity, budget and operating model. Suppliers should then answer a structured brief.

## 3. The recommended orchestration model

### 3.1 Be the owner's representative, not the manufacturer's broker

Setlhare Farms knows its land, history, preferences, assets and financial appetite. A manufacturer knows its own products, installation methods, technical requirements and price structures. An independent owner's representative helps the client navigate the difference between what can be bought and what should be developed.

Farouk's potential contribution is to:

- capture and clarify the owner's vision and investment constraints;
- coordinate collection of site, land, utility and operational information;
- arrange independent market and feasibility work;
- prepare the project brief and request-for-information documents;
- identify and prequalify potential designers, engineers, manufacturers and operators;
- organise comparable proposals rather than unstructured catalogues;
- compare installed and whole-life costs, safety, serviceability and site suitability;
- maintain a decision register, budget tracker, risk register and milestone plan;
- coordinate meetings and owner approvals;
- monitor contracted deliverables and handover readiness; and
- help develop a commercial calendar, packages and operating model with qualified advisers.

The owner retains the authority to approve budgets, designs, contracts, suppliers, construction and operations. Equipment suppliers should normally contract and invoice Setlhare Farms directly. The independent architect, engineer, aquatic consultant or other qualified professional should be appointed for the work that requires professional judgement, certification or statutory sign-off.

### 3.2 Separate three functions

**A. Owner-side orchestration (Farouk):** project definition, coordination, market research, procurement process administration, commercial analysis, reporting, risk tracking and implementation coordination within a written mandate.

**B. Independent professional design and assurance:** land-use planning, surveying, architecture, civil/structural engineering, mechanical/electrical engineering, water and wastewater design, aquatic engineering, environmental assessment, fire and life-safety design, accessibility, traffic/parking and other specialist work. These professionals must be appropriately qualified/registered where required and directly accountable for their designs and certifications.

**C. Product supply and installation:** manufacturers, authorised distributors, installers, contractors and specialist operators. They provide technical specifications, equipment, installation, commissioning, manuals, training, warranties, spares and maintenance arrangements.

A manufacturer can provide valuable early technical input, layouts and product-specific engineering. However, a supplier selling its own equipment is not automatically an independent masterplanner for the entire destination. Its recommendations should be compared with an independent owner-side concept and whole-site design.

### 3.3 Commercial safeguards and payment structure

Preferred structure:

1. **Paid discovery/brief assignment:** a short, written, fixed-scope engagement to review the map and available records, conduct an agreed site walk-through, interview the owner, identify constraints and produce a prioritised brief and next-stage scope.
2. **Separate feasibility/masterplanning commission:** only after the owner approves the scope, appoint the relevant independent planning and technical specialists, with each fee and deliverable visible to the owner.
3. **Owner-side development coordination retainer:** if the owner wants Farouk to continue, agree a fixed monthly fee for defined days, tasks, outputs and reporting. The existing BWP 10,000/month income objective remains a negotiation target for an agreed management/development mandate, not an amount already accepted by Setlhare Farms.
4. **Expenses:** travel, accommodation, printing, site investigations, specialists and project costs are owner-funded and approved in advance. Farouk should not finance project inputs or equipment personally.
5. **Procurement:** supplier prices and contracts should be visible to the owner; the owner approves and pays suppliers directly unless a different structure is expressly approved.
6. **Commissions:** best avoided for the initial mandate. If any supplier referral fee or commission is proposed, disclose the source, amount or calculation, timing and conflict in writing and obtain the owner's informed approval before any recommendation. No hidden margin or double-dipping.
7. **No profit-only substitute:** performance pay, turnover share or JV equity may be considered as additional upside, but should not replace payment for contracted work already performed.

A written appointment should state scope, deliverables, exclusions, authority limits, payment dates, reimbursable expenses, confidentiality, conflicts, insurance responsibilities, termination and dispute resolution. It should expressly exclude professional sign-off, engineering certification, construction supervision requiring a registered professional, safety certification, tourism operations, visitor bookings, ticket sales and handling customer money unless separately licensed, qualified and contracted.

### 3.4 Important Botswana tourism-licensing caution

The published Tourism Act text includes a broad restriction on carrying on or assisting in carrying on a tourist enterprise without a licence, and describes some activities such as negotiating, soliciting, canvassing or corresponding with prospective customers as part of carrying on an enterprise. The Act was under formal review in 2026, with Government describing proposed modernisation and product diversification. Before Farouk accepts a role beyond owner's-side development consulting and procurement coordination, the precise licensing position should be confirmed in writing with the Department of Tourism and, where appropriate, a Botswana legal practitioner. The wording of a private contract alone does not create a statutory exemption. Do not assume that a general project-coordination appointment authorises tourism sales, bookings, tour operation or visitor services. Sources: [Tourism Act text](https://www.laws.co.bw/download/tourism-board-act-chapter-4210/?filename=1681773446wpdm_TOURISM_Watermarked.pdf&ind=0&refresh=68a203ea948331755448298&wpdmdl=489) and [Government report on Tourism Act review, 23 June 2026](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/91947).

## 4. What kind of destination should Setlhare Farms become?

The strongest concept is not a conventional amusement park competing head-on with every large operator. It is a **multi-experience farm, leisure, recreation and events destination** that makes its existing conference, pool and garden assets more commercially useful while adding selected activities that suit the site and the market.

Potential visitor segments to test:

- families with children, especially weekend and school-holiday visitors;
- schools and educational groups;
- corporate teams, associations and staff events;
- birthday, graduation, reunion and family celebration groups;
- conference and meeting clients who want add-on recreation;
- couples and adult groups seeking outdoor games, social recreation and events;
- nature, gardening, wellness and environmental-learning groups;
- local community and regional visitors seeking a day out.

Do not assume there is enough paying demand for all these segments. Interview them and collect evidence before buying equipment.

### 4.1 Potential experience zones for screening

The described 60 m × 60 m areas would each equal approximately 3,600 m² if the dimensions are accurate. That is potentially useful for modular planning, but the exact location, shape, surveyed boundaries, road widths, topography and statutory status must be checked. A 3,600 m² area is not automatically a legally separate plot or a suitable attraction footprint.

The following are **concept options**, not approved allocations or procurement recommendations:

| Potential zone | Possible activities to assess | Main constraints |
|---|---|---|
| Aquatic play | splash pad, interactive water play, children's water features, pool upgrades | water rights/yield, treatment, drainage, energy, supervision, health requirements, lifecycle water cost |
| Adventure and challenge | ropes course, climbing elements, zipline, obstacle/ninja course | competent design, support structures, rescue plan, inspections, supervision, participant eligibility |
| Family play | inclusive playground, toddler zone, shaded play, low-height activities | age separation, impact attenuation, accessibility, supervision, heat/shade |
| Sport and games | mini-golf, lawn games, selected court sports, table games, team challenges | demand, surface, lighting, equipment replacement, operating staff |
| Nature and learning | gardens, indigenous-plant trail, bird/ecology interpretation, guided educational activities | water-efficient landscaping, interpretation quality, conservation and land-use compatibility |
| Events and social | outdoor stage, picnic lawn, pop-up market, food stalls, small performances | noise, crowd management, sanitation, waste, fire access, licensing, weather |
| Existing facilities hub | conferencing, pool, gardens, catering and event packages | current condition, capacity, accessibility, toilets, insurance, staffing, bookings and maintenance |

Use the existing conference, pool and garden assets as the initial destination hub. Shared services—entrance, reception, parking, toilets, first aid, security, waste handling, food service, shaded waiting, accessible paths, utilities and emergency access—should be planned once for the whole property. This will be more efficient than treating every activity area as an isolated business.

### 4.2 "Adult entertainment" should mean age-appropriate recreation

For this project, define adult activities as wholesome, lawful, inclusive leisure and social recreation rather than gambling or adult-oriented sexual entertainment. Potential options include lawn games, social sports, fitness challenges, team competitions, mini-golf, walking trails, garden dining, music and cultural events, outdoor cinema, workshops and corporate challenges. Activities should be suitable for the owner's chosen brand, local community expectations, safety controls and the intended audience.

Where adults and children share the destination, define age zones, supervision, access rules and event schedules. Do not place high-speed, high-risk or alcohol-led activities next to children's play or school programmes. Keep events, quiet gardens, conference functions and high-noise attractions separated in the site plan.

## 5. Market context and competitive positioning

Two relevant local competitors make it clear that “a place with games” is not by itself a differentiated proposition.

- **Africa Plays, Gaborone** currently advertises indoor bowling, ninja course, trampoline park and soft play, with birthday, family, school/university and corporate/team-building products. It promotes monitored activity zones and participation waivers. This is a directly relevant family-entertainment benchmark. [Official site](https://africaplays.co.bw/).
- **Lion Park Resort** advertises water attractions and rides, children's play, and other attractions, with published general access tickets and group packages. It is an established outdoor leisure benchmark. [Official tickets](https://lionpark.co.bw/tickets/) and [packages](https://lionpark.co.bw/our-packages/).

A Setlhare Farms proposition should therefore compete through its combination of outdoor space, gardens, events, conferencing, selected nature experiences, family recreation and group packages. It may also have an advantage in being able to phase modular activities rather than build a single indoor facility. This advantage is only real if visitor access, travel distance, sanitation, water, staffing, shade, safety and operating cost are workable.

Botswana's National Tourism Strategy and Government's 2026 public statements support product diversification, citizen participation and development of cultural, heritage, eco-tourism and community-based tourism. This is a policy direction, not proof of demand or a grant commitment. See [Government report, 23 June 2026](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/91947).

### 5.1 Market validation before capex

Conduct structured discovery with:
- 10–15 families across different spending levels;
- 5–8 schools or educational/youth groups;
- 5–8 corporate HR, events or team-building buyers;
- 5 event planners/caterers/venue users;
- existing Setlhare Farms customers and conference users, if the owner permits access;
- a small group of local tourism, recreation and community stakeholders.

Questions should test actual behaviour and purchasing:
- What do they currently visit, how often and how far do they travel?
- What is missing from existing venues?
- Which activities would prompt a visit to this specific site?
- What group size, duration and day of week do they need?
- What would they realistically pay per person or per group?
- Would they pay for a package or only for individual attractions?
- What are the safety, hygiene, food, shade, accessibility and transport expectations?
- Would they book in advance, and what deposit/payment arrangements are acceptable?

Separate stated interest ("sounds exciting") from evidence of willingness to pay (a written expression of interest, booking enquiry, conditional group booking or paid pilot). Do not treat social-media likes or manufacturers' attendance forecasts as market proof.

## 6. Phased development roadmap

### Phase 0 — Project definition and site due diligence

**Purpose:** Establish what can legally, physically and commercially be developed before selecting attractions.

Required work:
1. Obtain the site map, survey diagram, land title/lease/land-board documents, parcel references and all existing development plans.
2. Clarify whether 60 m × 60 m sections are surveyed cadastral subdivisions, approved lease areas, internal development blocks or only a conceptual layout.
3. Conduct a site walk-through with Bernard and the relevant Setlhare Farms decision-maker.
4. Commission a topographical/site survey, boundary and access verification, geotechnical inputs where structures require them, and utility mapping as appropriate.
5. Record existing buildings, pools, gardens, parking, fences, roads, drainage, power, water points, boreholes, pumps, tanks, sewer/septic arrangements and waste facilities.
6. Establish water source, permitted use, yield, quality, storage, treatment, pumping cost, seasonal reliability and potential reuse. Major aquatic attractions are not to be designed from a guessed water supply.
7. Assess road access, visibility, public transport, parking, emergency-vehicle access, pedestrian safety and internal circulation.
8. Review current permitted land use, applicable planning controls, development permissions, change-of-use requirements, environmental assessment, tourism licensing, public health, fire, building, trade licensing and any relevant standards.
9. Collect available data on visitor history, conference occupancy, event revenue, operating expenses, staff, incidents, insurance and maintenance.
10. Agree the owner's capital ceiling, preferred funding model, risk appetite, operating partner preference and project governance.

**Phase 0 deliverable:** A concise feasibility and project-definition report identifying constraints, opportunity, information gaps, potential phases, preliminary costs to investigate and a go/no-go recommendation. No major equipment purchase.

### Phase 1 — Activate existing assets and test customer demand

Use existing conference, pool and gardens where they are legally approved, safe, serviceable and operationally ready. Possible offers to test include:
- conference plus garden lunch or light recreation;
- family pool-and-garden day package;
- birthdays and family celebrations;
- corporate team-building using temporary/mobile games;
- school environmental-learning days and guided garden activities;
- small seasonal markets, cultural or outdoor family events;
- garden photography and private functions;
- catering/event packages through appropriately licensed providers.

This phase needs an event calendar, price architecture, booking and cancellation conditions, staffing plan, sanitation, first aid, security, crowd limits, maintenance and incident procedures. Do not start selling packages until the responsible operator confirms the licensing and public-health position.

A low-cost pilot using temporary, hired or modular activities can reveal attendance, spend per visitor, labour intensity, customer satisfaction, weather effects and repeat demand. Temporary equipment is not automatically exempt from safety or legal requirements.

### Phase 2 — First permanent attraction nodes

Select no more than one or two nodes after demand and site feasibility evidence. Prioritise options with:
- moderate initial capital;
- clear local buyer demand;
- low utility and maintenance burden;
- manageable safety exposure;
- robust warranties and accessible parts/service;
- good fit with existing conference/pool/garden users;
- ability to expand modularly.

A shaded, inclusive family-play area or a defined active-games/adventure feature might warrant investigation before a large water-park investment, but neither should be selected until a proper comparison is done. A concession or specialist operator may be preferable to direct purchase if it transfers appropriate operating responsibilities and provides measurable service standards.

### Phase 3 — Aquatic and higher-capital attractions

Consider major water slides, larger splash structures, wave or surf attractions, extensive pools or rides only after:
- water rights and sustainable yield are verified;
- water treatment, filtration, drainage and reuse are designed;
- electricity and wastewater capacity are confirmed;
- independent aquatic/civil/mechanical/electrical design is completed;
- land-use, building, environmental, public-health and tourism approvals are confirmed;
- capex, imported freight, duties, installation, civil works, commissioning, insurance, staffing and annual maintenance are included;
- a credible funding and cash-flow plan is agreed;
- emergency response, supervision, inspection and insurance arrangements are operational.

A product quote is not a project budget. The total installed and lifecycle cost is what matters.

### Phase 4 — Mature destination and operating model

Only after the initial phases prove demand should Setlhare Farms add complementary experiences, events, concessions and possible accommodation/food-service units. Each new unit should have a defined operator, service level, cost centre, maintenance plan and contribution to the overall guest experience. Do not maximise the number of activities at the expense of reliability, safety or profitability.

## 8. Botswana planning, water, tourism and safety workstream

This section is a project-risk checklist, not a legal opinion. The site address, tenure, land-use zone and latest applicable legislation still need confirmation.

### 8.1 Land use and subdivision

A 9 March 2026 Parliament response stated that Botswana had been declared a planning area countrywide in August 2023, and described the Spatial Development Code 2025, including development-intensity zones and a Mixed Use Agriculture approach that may allow complementary non-agricultural uses in a defined proportion. The same statement described change-of-use processes involving publication, public representations and relevant Land Board or lessor consent. This must be verified against the actual site zoning and current legal instruments before investment. [Parliament Hansard, 9 March 2026](https://www.parliament.gov.bw/documents/DAILY-HANSARD--09-MARCH-2026---Budget-Meeting--2nd-meeting-of-the-2nd-session-of-the-13th-Parliament_11_07_59_16_03_2026.pdf).

Botswana's Agro-Tourism Guidelines also describe land-use and infrastructure requirements, including restrictions for prime agricultural land and National Agricultural Production Zones. The published guide is older than the Spatial Development Code 2025. Its stated percentages should not be applied mechanically without the planning authority confirming which rule governs the specific parcel today. [Agro-Tourism Guidelines](https://www.gov.bw/sites/default/files/2020-03/AGRO-TOURISM-GUIDELINES.pdf).

First establish whether the 60 m × 60 m areas are legally surveyed subdivisions or only internal activity zones. Roads, parcel boundaries and separate business units may require planning and survey approvals. Do not assume a physical partition authorises a change of land use or an independent commercial enterprise.

### 8.2 Environmental assessment

The Government's environmental-assessment procedure identifies project information, land documentation and location information as part of the process. The applicable level of assessment must be determined by the competent authority based on the actual development. Early consultation is preferable before committing to earthworks, water features, major structures or tree/landscape changes. [Environmental Assessment authorisation](https://www.gov.bw/environmental-protection/environmental-assessment-authorisation).

### 8.3 Water, pools and public health

Before any new aquatic attraction, establish the lawful water source and water-use permissions, abstraction yield, quality, storage, treatment, recirculation, drainage and wastewater capacity. The Government has a permit process covering drawing water from boreholes, wells, dams, lakes and rivers. [Water abstraction permission](https://www.gov.bw/water-and-sanitation/apply-permission-draw-water).

Swimming pools and aquatic play also need competent design, water-quality management, testing, plant maintenance, lifeguard/supervision arrangements, incident procedures, public-health review and appropriate insurance. Confirm current requirements with the relevant health authority and Botswana Bureau of Standards (BOBS); do not rely on a vendor's claim of compliance without seeing the exact standards and evidence.

### 8.4 Tourism, trade and activity-specific licences

The Tourism Act review was active during 2026. The official public statement described the objective of promoting product diversification, ecotourism and improved business conditions, but a review announcement does not itself amend the law. Confirm the current law and the licence categories applicable to the owner, operator and Farouk's proposed role before offering or selling tourism products. [Government Tourism Act review](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/91947).

Trade, food, public-health, liquor (if any), entertainment, event, fire, building and other approvals may depend on the specific activity, operator and site. Do not assume a conference venue approval covers a water-play park, ropes course, food service or other new attraction. Confirm in writing with the responsible authorities.

### 8.5 Safety and insurance

Each attraction requires an activity-specific risk assessment and operating system, not just a general indemnity waiver. At minimum address:
- design review and structural/installation verification;
- safe operating limits, age/height restrictions and participant screening;
- trained operators and supervision;
- emergency access, first aid, rescue and incident reporting;
- daily pre-opening checks and planned inspections;
- preventive maintenance, defect isolation and closure authority;
- visitor information, rules and consent/guardian processes;
- safeguarding and separation of children from incompatible activities;
- accessible routes and inclusive participation where feasible;
- fire safety, occupancy limits, evacuation and crowd movement;
- public liability, employer and asset insurance appropriate to each activity.

For rides and adventure activities, request recognised international technical standards and independent inspection evidence appropriate to the actual attraction type. IAAPA provides industry safety resources, but the competent Botswana authorities and qualified local professionals must determine the applicable legal requirements and adopted standards. [IAAPA safety resources](https://www.iaapa.org/resources/safety-security).

## 9. Commercial model and unit economics

Each attraction or activity area should be evaluated as a separate cost centre and as part of the whole destination. Build a simple model before committing:
- installed capital cost, including imported freight/duties, foundations, utilities, access, shade, fencing, design, installation, commissioning and contingency;
- financing cost and payment timing;
- annual fixed costs: staffing, insurance, licences, security, power, water, administration, marketing and maintenance;
- variable cost per visitor or booking;
- realistic operating days and weather/seasonality;
- safe and practical capacity per hour/day;
- expected paid attendance, not theoretical maximum throughput;
- ticket/package price and discounts;
- group booking, event hire, catering, sponsorship and concession revenue;
- repairs, consumables, periodic component replacement and downtime;
- cash reserve and working capital;
- contribution margin and break-even attendance.

A basic attraction contribution calculation is:

**Contribution per paid use = net realised price per use − variable cost per use.**

**Break-even paid uses = annual fixed operating costs ÷ contribution per paid use.**

This excludes capital recovery unless the capital charge is added to annual fixed cost. All model assumptions must be labelled as owner-provided, quoted, observed or estimated. Use low/base/high scenarios and stress-test low attendance, water/power increases, downtime and repair events. Avoid a single optimistic forecast.

Potential operating forms:
1. Owner-operated: more control and potential margin, but requires trained staff, maintenance discipline and active management.
2. Specialist concession: operator funds or operates a defined activity and pays a fixed rent or agreed turnover share; the agreement needs performance standards, insurance, maintenance, auditability and termination/closure rights.
3. Revenue-share partnership: only where revenue reporting and cost responsibilities can be independently checked.
4. Direct procurement and owner operation: viable only when the owner has sufficient capital and capacity to manage the asset.

An equipment manufacturer should not be assumed to fund, operate or maintain an attraction merely because it sells it. Any financing, concession or operator arrangement must be explicit and assessed separately.

### 16.1 Request the whole-property plan, not only the activity blocks

If available, also request the **survey diagram/general plan** and the property's land-rights document (for example, title deed, lease or relevant Land Board certificate/records). These are different documents and serve different purposes:

- **Survey diagram / general plan:** cadastral land information, such as the surveyed parcel, boundaries, dimensions and survey references. This is the document family to use when checking the legal extent and identity of the land.
- **Location map:** shows where the property is situated in relation to roads, settlements and surrounding features.
- **Site plan / existing development layout:** shows the arrangement of the property, access, roads, buildings, services and other site features. This is the most useful initial drawing for understanding how the destination currently functions.
- **Approved development or planning layout:** where one exists, records the development arrangement accepted by the relevant planning authority. It should be distinguished from an informal concept sketch or an owner's internal activity layout.

The reported 60 m × 60 m areas must not be described as legal subdivisions unless the survey and land records establish that status.
